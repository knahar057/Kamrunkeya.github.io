Kamrun Nahar Keya

School of Mathematical and Statistical Sciences, Arizona State University

Research

My research lies at the intersection of computational mathematics, numerical linear algebra, and optimization. I am particularly interested in developing efficient algorithms for large-scale and dynamically changing data, with an emphasis on low-rank matrix and tensor methods. My research also includes data-driven mathematical modeling and applications to biological systems.

Current Research

Fast Updating of Low-Rank Matrix Factorizations

I am developing efficient algorithms for updating low-rank matrix factorizations when a matrix undergoes changes or receives new data. The goal is to update an existing factorization efficiently rather than recomputing it from scratch, particularly for large-scale problems.

Online Low-Rank Matrix Factorization

I study low-rank matrix factorization in online and streaming settings, where data arrive sequentially and the underlying factorization must be updated as new information becomes available. This work focuses on optimization techniques for efficiently maintaining low-rank representations of evolving data.

Randomized Tensor Decomposition

I am investigating randomized algorithms for canonical polyadic (CP) tensor decomposition. The goal is to develop scalable computational methods for extracting low-rank structure from large multidimensional datasets.

Mathematical Modeling of Population Dynamics

My earlier research focused on mathematical models of population dynamics and biological systems. This work included data-driven discrete models for flour beetle populations as well as models involving population dispersal, diffusion, competition, harvesting, and Allee effects.

In particular, I studied a data-induced two-population model for Tribolium confusum, investigating its dynamics and its agreement with experimental population data.
